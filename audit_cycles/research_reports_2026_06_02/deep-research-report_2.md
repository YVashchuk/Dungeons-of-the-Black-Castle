## Methodology

I treated `book_text.md` as the canonical text source, exactly as requested, and `remake_data.js` as the source of truth for spell hooks in the live build. I used `game_logic.js` to determine how `spell`-tagged choices are actually consumed and how combat-modal spells are exposed. The engine declares 8 spells in `SPELLS`, consumes spell charges through `useSpell(spellId)`, applies `FORCE` and `WEAKNESS` only through the combat-button functions `useForceInCombat()` / `useWeaknessInCombat()`, and hides the healing HUD button whenever the combat modal is open. fileciteturn0file2

Parsing rule: a paragraph counts for a spell if the canon text explicitly gives the player a chance to cast that spell in that paragraph. I therefore **excluded** result-only paragraphs such as spell aftermath nodes, lore mentions such as warnings or enemy counter-magic, and generic unnamed phrases like `«Заклятиями можете пользоваться по своему усмотрению»` when no specific spell is named. This matters because the live engine currently defaults most unrestricted combats to `COPY` / `FORCE` / `WEAKNESS` buttons, which would massively inflate a raw “UI exposure” count beyond the canon text. The table below is therefore a **canonical explicit-use table**, not a raw UI-button table. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file3

Assumption note: the spell identifiers are explicit in `game_logic.js` (`LEVITATION`, `FIRE`, `ILLUSION`, `FORCE`, `WEAKNESS`, `COPY`, `HEALING`, `SWIMMING`). No naming ambiguity remained at the identifier level. The only substantive ambiguities were paragraph-structure ambiguities, especially where one spell-gated sentence fans out into multiple sibling destinations; those are called out separately as findings. fileciteturn0file2

## Verification findings

1. **P1 — Combat-modal default currently inflates `COPY` / `FORCE` / `WEAKNESS` far beyond canon.**  
   The engine default is `const allowedSpells = sec.combat_spells_allowed || ['COPY','FORCE','WEAKNESS'];`, so any combat paragraph without an explicit allowlist gets those three buttons in the combat modal. That is not text-faithful in several paragraphs that explicitly forbid magic or say there is no time to cast. Canon examples include §58 `«Воспользоваться заклятиями уже не успеваете.»`, §617 `«вы не успеваете применить заклятия»`, §618 `«Заклятия не помогут — Призрак не боится их.»`, §823 `«Вы не можете воспользоваться ни одним заклятием»`, and §994 `«Заклятия вам не помогут.»` Yet unless a paragraph was specially allowlisted, the current engine still grants `COPY` / `FORCE` / `WEAKNESS` buttons there. This is the key reason I did **not** use raw current UI exposure as the spell-usage count. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file3

2. **P1 — §950 breaks a canonical `HEALING` hook.**  
   The canon is explicit: `«Правда, пару заклятий вы можете использовать: это заклятие Силы и Исцеления»`. In current data, §950 only allows `FORCE`, and in the engine the healing HUD button is hidden whenever the combat modal is open. So `HEALING` is canonically usable at §950 but practically unavailable in the current build. Since `HEALING` has only two canonical explicit-use paragraphs total, this one missing hook materially distorts its valuation. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file3

3. **P1 — Navigation-style `FORCE` / `WEAKNESS` branches do not carry their effect into combat, and several also double-spend.**  
   The engine only applies the actual +2 / −2 combat effect inside `useForceInCombat()` and `useWeaknessInCombat()`. By contrast, the older paragraph-chain branches consume charges through ordinary `spell`-tagged navigation, and there is no persistent “pending pre-combat buff/debuff” field that survives into the target fight. Canon examples are straightforward: §308 says `«Прибавьте 2 к своей СИЛЕ УДАРА и сражайтесь (1175) — заклятие Силы подействовало»`; §470 says `«Вычитайте 2 из СИЛЫ УДАРА одного из гоблинов, когда будете сражаться — 536»`; §865 / §1088 are reflection branches from §96 that should resolve the spell effect textually before the fight restarts. In the current engine, those states are not persisted as combat modifiers, and some chains even spend the spell twice: §208→§308→§1175, §449→§192→§532, §536→§286→§536, §468→§160→§46, §96→§865→§96, and §449→§1183→§532. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file3

4. **P1 — Two explicit `FIRE` hooks are currently wrong in data.**  
   The first is §700: the canon offers `«Огня(1188) или драться мечом (628)»`, but the current data has only one non-spell choice to §628 and no `spell:"FIRE"` path to §1188. The second is §283: the canon says `«Хотите наложить заклятие Огня (779)»`, but the current choice is tagged `ILLUSION`, which misclassifies the paragraph and distorts both the Fire and Illusion tables. These are not design notes; they are direct hook-integrity problems. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file3

5. **P1 — Multi-destination movement hooks are only partially gated.**  
   This is the cleanest later-use-style spell-gating cluster in the file. In §131 the canon says `«Если у вас есть заклятие Левитации, то можете подняться по воздуху вверх (836), или в какое-нибудь окно ... справа (907) ... слева (1075)»`, but only §836 is actually spell-gated. In §980 the canon similarly conditions four flight destinations on Levitation, but only the first is gated. In §402 the canon says `«еще одно заклятие Левитации или Плавания (641)»`, but only Levitation is accepted. In §614 the canon says `«Плавания или Левитации»`, but only Swimming is accepted. In §944 the first Swimming crossing `«Плавания (1217)»` lacks `spell:"SWIMMING"`, and in §951 the first Swimming crossing `«Плавания (1021)»` likewise lacks it. These are all direct spell-hook mismatches between canon and GD. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file3

6. **P3 — Balance read after the full sweep.**  
   On canonical explicit-use coverage alone, `LEVITATION` is the most broadly reused utility spell, `COPY` is the most repeatedly named combat spell, and `FIRE` / `FORCE` / `WEAKNESS` all sit in a strong middle tier. `ILLUSION` is genuinely sparse at only 4 explicit-use paragraphs, which confirms the longstanding suspicion that it is close to dead weight. `HEALING` is even lower on paragraph count, but its low count is partially an implementation artifact because §950 is missing. If §950 were fixed, `HEALING` would still be niche, but less misleadingly so. Meanwhile the current combat-default behavior makes `COPY` / `FORCE` / `WEAKNESS` much stronger in practice than the canon paragraph distribution alone would suggest. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file3

## Balance interpretation

If I value the spell list by **canonical explicit-use coverage**, the rough tiers are clear. The strongest coverage belongs to `LEVITATION`, then `COPY`, then `FIRE`, followed closely by `FORCE` and `WEAKNESS`. `SWIMMING` is moderately narrow but still materially relevant because it gates several large geography transitions. `ILLUSION` is the true low-frequency spell. `HEALING` is low-frequency in paragraph text but has a separate engine-wide non-combat utility because the HUD lets the player spend it whenever wounded and out of combat. That global utility prevents it from being literally dead, but it does not erase the fact that it has only two explicit canonical paragraph hooks, one of which is currently broken. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2

Accordingly, my P3 balance note is this: **within canon, `ILLUSION` is the weakest pick; within the current engine, `COPY` / `FORCE` / `WEAKNESS` are artificially overpowered because of unrestricted combat defaults; and `HEALING` is paradoxically both niche in paragraph design and still useful in everyday play because of its HUD-level availability.** That means a player optimizing the present build is not optimizing the book’s intended spell economy. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file3
