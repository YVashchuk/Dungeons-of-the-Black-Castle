## **Introduction to the 1991 First Edition and the Material Context of Ergodic Literature**

The landscape of interactive fiction, branching narratives, and ergodic literature in Eastern Europe is fundamentally anchored by Dmitry Braslavsky’s seminal work, «Подземелья Чёрного замка» (Dungeons of the Black Castle). First published in 1991 by the "Детская литература" (Children's Literature) publishing house, this text represents the absolute genesis of the Russian gamebook (книга-игра) genre. The exigency of establishing a definitive, source-critical restoration of the 1991 first edition cannot be overstated. Over the decades, subsequent printings, unauthorized digital adaptations, community-driven remakes, and modern commercial remasters—most notably the heavily revised modern FB2 formats—have systematically altered the topological graph, lexical composition, mechanical equilibrium, and overall pacing of the original text. The publishing context of the Soviet Union in 1991 and the immediate post-Soviet transition period introduced unique material conditions that directly impacted the physical artifact of the gamebook. Widespread paper shortages, erratic typesetting practices at provincial printing presses, and the complete absence of established editing conventions for non-linear literature resulted in a text that is both historically invaluable and structurally idiosyncratic.  
A source-critical restoration must isolate the authentic 1991 state of the text, preserving its mechanical anomalies, distinct lexical choices, typographical errors, and topological dead-ends, rather than yielding to the temptation of retrospective correction. The analysis indicates that modern digital editions have sanitized the prose and fundamentally rewired the underlying mathematical graph to conform to contemporary expectations of game design, frequently implementing automated state tracking and auto-mapping features that destroy the raw, manual experience intended by the author. Consequently, establishing the true 1991 text demands an exhaustive mapping of verbatim text strings, anchored firmly by primary evidence extracted from historical walkthroughs («прохождение») and early internet text archives. The target is the original artifact, preserving the exact ludic and narrative parameters established by Braslavsky at the dawn of the medium.  
The historical significance of «Подземелья Чёрного замка» extends beyond its structural innovation; it encapsulates a unique synthesis of Western fantasy tropes—heavily influenced by the Fighting Fantasy series created by Steve Jackson and Ian Livingstone—and Eastern European literary traditions. The text operates as a transitional artifact, bridging the gap between passive reading and interactive ludic engagement for a generation of readers encountering this medium for the first time. The 1991 edition is characterized by a specific tonal register, blending earnest high fantasy with occasionally abrupt, utilitarian prose necessary to navigate the complex conditional logic of the gamebook's mechanics. The critical effort to restore this text is a foundational project in digital heritage and media archaeology. To approach the 1991 text requires abandoning the convenience of modern paragraph numbering systems, which have proven highly volatile across editions, and instead adopting a rigorous philological framework rooted in verbatim scene matching and the critical analysis of contemporaneous player traversal records.

## **Theoretical Framework for the Rejection of Numerical Anchoring**

The restoration of branching narratives presents unique philological and mathematical challenges that deviate significantly from the critical editing of linear texts. In a conventional novel, the physical sequentiality of paragraphs remains relatively stable across editions, allowing editors to reference chapters or page numbers with a high degree of confidence. However, in an ergodic text—where the sequence of reading is determined by reader choice, stochastic mechanical variables, and conditional logic—the numbered paragraphs function not merely as textual divisions, but as vertices within a directed mathematical graph. The edges of this graph are the explicit choices offered at the conclusion of each textual unit.  
The structural analysis of the multiple editions of «Подземелья Чёрного замка» spanning from 1991 to the present day reveals a persistent, highly destructive pattern of editorial intervention where nodes have been renumbered, deleted, conflated, or entirely rewritten to optimize pagination, correct perceived logical errors, or accommodate different physical book formats. Because the structure is a directed graph, changing the numerical identifier of a single vertex requires updating every single edge (choice) that points to that vertex. In early publishing environments, this updating process was frequently flawed, leading to broken links, orphan nodes (paragraphs that cannot be reached), and dead ends (paragraphs from which there is no exit). Consequently, relying on paragraph numbers as navigational anchors across different editions is a methodologically bankrupt approach. A paragraph numbered "250" in the 1991 edition may correspond to paragraph "253" in a 1995 reprint, and might be completely absent or merged in a modern FB2 remaster. Therefore, a critical restoration must enforce an absolute, non-negotiable prohibition against matching paragraphs across editions by number.  
The only rigorous, mathematically sound methodology for establishing a definitive textual lineage in ergodic literature is verbatim scene matching. This approach treats the exact text string itself—rather than its volatile numerical index—as the primary, immutable unit of analysis. By extracting distinct narrative sequences, dialogic exchanges, atmospheric descriptions, and specific mechanical commands, and mapping them against historical sources, the original topography of the 1991 graph can be reconstructed entirely independently of any subsequent editorial renumbering. This methodology is particularly crucial when addressing the «Подземелья Чёрного замка» graph, which relies heavily on hub-and-spoke mechanics, where a central room (such as the dungeon's main antechamber or the Great Hall) provides multiple recurring return paths. If a single node in this hub is altered or renumbered in a subsequent edition, the entire mathematical matrix of the surrounding text is corrupted, leading to cascading structural failures.  
The reliance on textual invariants—specific, highly idiosyncratic phrases that remain unaltered despite the mechanical changes occurring around them—is the cornerstone of this process. The Russian language, with its highly inflected grammar, flexible syntax, and rich vocabulary, offers numerous distinct lexical combinations that serve as unique, cryptographic identifiers for any given scene. When analyzing the transition from the 1991 print to subsequent formats, the tracking of these verbatim invariants exposes the precise boundaries of editorial sanitization. The core objective is to strip away the accretions, corrections, and digital "improvements" of the past three decades, isolating the raw, mechanically complex, structurally perilous, and historically authentic text that emerged from the printing press in 1991\.

By analyzing the specific variables tracked in these archival walkthroughs—specifically the management of the core statistics: Мастерство (Skill), Выносливость (Stamina), and Удача (Luck)—the mechanical equilibrium of the 1991 edition can be quantified and verified. However, the source-critical restoration demands the absolute reinstatement of these punishing original mechanics. The walkthroughs validate the existence and exact parameters of these original, unnerfed encounters, providing the verbatim text of the failure states and the required statistics that characterize the true 1991 experience.  
Furthermore, walkthroughs frequently capture verbatim snippets of text used to justify spatial movement or item acquisition.
